date: "2026-07-02"

---

Most audits chase the big-ticket line items — the ERP contract, the data center lease, the headline vendor. That's where the attention goes, and it's not wrong to look there. But it's rarely where the money actually leaks.

The real waste tends to hide in smaller, quieter places:

- Renewal cycles that auto-renew because nobody put a date on the calendar.
- Per-seat licenses purchased for a team that shrank two reorgs ago.
- Redundant tools doing the same job because procurement happened in two different departments, two different years.

None of these show up on a slide with a big number next to it. They show up one invoice at a time, and by the time anyone notices, the pattern has been running for years.

This is the part of an IT spend audit that pays for itself fastest — not because the fixes are dramatic, but because nobody was looking.
